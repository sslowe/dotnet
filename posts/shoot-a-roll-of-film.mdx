At long last, we have arrived at the tipping point in the Endeavor, with fewer tasks in front than behind. Way behind schedule, mind you, but better late than never. Feel free to jump [back to the beginning](/posts/document-everything) if you’re just joining us.

You know how some kids collect bobbleheads, Pez dispensers, what have you? It was a running joke in our family that I was a collector of collections, a sort of meta-collector, if you will. Bobbleheads? Check. Pez dispensers? Check. Trading cards? Check. WebKinz? You bet your bottom dollar that’s a big ol’ check. I could go on, but I think you get the idea. In retrospect, I’ve come to realize that my impulse was less about collecting and more about preservation. Maybe it’s because I came into my family on the tail end of a generation, who’s to say? But what I do know is that my desire to catalog and archive translated pretty cleanly into an early interest in photography. 

Back before everyone had a camera in their pocket, families always had a designated photographer or two, and ours was no different. ~~Mother~~ editor on the other side of her Canon Rebel is an iconic image of my childhood, and my aunt, Gaynell, still has the best eye for a photo out of anyone I know. While I make no claim on being anything more than a casual hobbyist, they both helped teach me the joy of capturing a moment for posterity.

Growing up in the final days of analog media, I’ve always been fascinated by film and, like pretty much everyone else in my age bracket with a moderate artsy streak, got *really* into Fujifilm Instax in college. The natural next step is getting into 35mm cameras, and many of my friends were carrying around disposables by senior year. I opted for a reloadable point-and-shoot, picking up a beater of a Canon Prima Super 120 in Berlin during my semester studying abroad in London. 

Given the convenience and ever-increasing fidelity of the cameras we all carry in our pockets, it’s quite easy to let lapse the kind of deliberateness it takes to lug around a standalone camera. For my part, the last roll of film I got developed from my camera was the one I shot over that semester, so the Endeavor felt like an ideal excuse to get back to it. Imagine my surprise, then, to find a roll already loaded and a little under half used, seemingly from my time at UNC. One of the greatest joys of film is the delayed gratification of seeing how your shots turned out (or didn't). I can confirm that it hits even harder when you’ve forgotten what you photographed in the first place.

I present to you a gallery of two eras: a little of life then, a little of life now. And if you haven’t yet, let this be a reminder to take a photo today. Your future self will thank you.
