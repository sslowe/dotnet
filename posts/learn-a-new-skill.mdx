Today’s task took a winding journey on its way to getting crossed off the list. After making my original cut at the outset of the Endeavor, “Learn a new skill" was ultimately replaced by an alternate for reasons I’ll be getting into shortly. It got reinstated, however, thanks to a recent AskReddit thread about skills that take less than an hour to learn. Hey, sometimes you need low-hanging fruit to balance out the kind of effort it takes to design an [entire card game](/posts/fabricate-a-product).

One of my favorite parts of working with [Router](/router) was that, while design was always the throughline, each new project meant putting on a new hat to learn whatever tools were needed to get that particular job done. Take the Pinegrove [Mapster](https://www.mapster.website/) project for example. I invested a great deal of time at the outset of that project devising a method to accurately pin coordinates over a hand painted map. My search required me to identify the right software library for the job ([Leaflet](https://leafletjs.com/), if you’re curious) and, as it turned out, to fall down a sizable rabbit hole on map projections, which culminated with the purchase of a physical atlas to decipher the projection parameters of the original painting. <span className="text-[#77FFE4] text-sm">[And even then, we had to get creative in Photoshop.]</span>

Why take the task off the list if learning new skills and researching new domains is so enjoyable to me? The realization that the incentive structure for skill acquisition has shifted massively in the agentic coding era, as least as far as it concerns the digital realm. I still like to explore new tools and libraries, but there’s this strange inversion that has taken place where the learning happens from the doing rather than precedes it. What’s more, is that agentic coding makes the tools you already *do* know flexible in ways that might not have been practical before. Taken in sum, this new landscape pushes digital skill acquisition into more of an applied practice in service of an end ([walking brainstorm systems](/posts/engineer-an-ai-system), perhaps?), rather than an end in itself.

That realization left me in the position of needing some sort of physical, real-world skill to learn for the task. Having none readily occur to me, off the task went from the list. Until, that is, I was reminded of a present that I had yet to take proper advantage of. The skill I decided to learn? Oh, it’s *totally* harmless. A party trick, really.

## Nefarious? Me? Never!

As the [excellent video essay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sz0Szo1UKRE) by Eddy and Tony Burback attests, my generation grew up on peak spy media. Spy Kids. Agent Cody Banks. Kim Possible. Totally Spies. It was the basis for an entire line of shoddily-produced electronic toys. It was inescapable. A side effect of this inundation is that many of us have a persistent thought at the back of our minds that one day, just maybe, a mysterious figure will appear and recruit us for a top-secret mission to topple a shadowy cabal. It’s why [Cicada 3301](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicada_3301) still lives rent-free in my head. Sometimes, it manifests in the acquisition of objects or abilities of little practical use. <span className="text-[#77FFE4] text-sm">[It’s not just me, right?]</span>

This is one of those times. At least, that’s the best explanation I can muster as to why I learned rudimentary lock picking that won’t upset ~~mother~~ editor. The basics of picking pin tumblers can indeed be learned in under an hour, but like any hobby, the fundamentals bely a great deal of complexity. If you’ve never happened upon it before, I highly recommend checking out [LockPickingLawyer](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCm9K6rby98W8JigLoZOh6FQ) on YouTube (and if you’re short on time, I recommend at least watching one of his top-tier April Fool’s Day videos). It’s one of those special channels that manages to imbue a niche topic with broad appeal (like my guy [Steve1989MREInfo](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC2I6Et1JkidnnbWgJFiMeHA)).

So to any international spy agencies reading this: hit me up. I’ve got the skills. For everyone else, if this is my last post, you know what happened.
