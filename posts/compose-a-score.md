## Transparent Tuning

It’s no secret that I like a quality walk. For one, you don’t move to Brooklyn if you’re averse to the notion, and further, you certainly wouldn’t engineer a [walking brainstorm system](/posts/engineer-an-ai-system) unless you’re a practitioner. There are any number of walks that you become well-versed in when living in the city, like the “hurry-up-there’s-a-train”, the “slow-down-there-will-be-another”, or the “oh-my-god-it’s-finally-not-{freezing, roasting}-and-I-want-to-be-outside.” What’s true of most walks of the solo variety is that they’re often improved by music. 

Music preference is a matter of taste, of course, but some walks lend themselves better to certain types of music than others. For example, I’ve always tended towards instrumentals when brainstorming because lyrics draw too much focus. And then there’s the matter of listening arrangement. One AirPod or two? Noise-cancellation? I enjoy experiencing the city around me, so I usually opt for one in, one out, but it’s hard to argue that you’re not losing something when you go down to mono. 

It was on a walk to the [Brooklyn Botanical Garden](https://www.bbg.org/) that I came up with a unique solution, but one that only works for certain types of music: listening on a low volume with transparency turned on, letting the music and background blend into one amalgam. My initial experiment (and still one of my favorites to do this with) was with Sigur Rós, though there are many ideal candidates. 

As opposed to repurposing existing music for this new paradigm, I thought it would make for an interesting prompt to attempt to compose directly for that listening environment, with the additional benefit that it pairs particularly well with my [MindWalk](https://github.com/sslowe/mindwalk) system. That’s the impetus behind the descriptively titled *Music for Transparency*. The piece is site-unspecified music that becomes [site-specific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site-specific_art) with every listen. 

It’s been a while since I tried my hand at a score, the last attempt being a collaboration with my Router cofounder, Scott, for the third installation of his [Resurrection Machine](https://informationarcade.com/resurrection/) series. It’s a closely-related but distinct skillset to songwriting that uses alternate approaches for similar ends, albeit on different time scales.

Back in my time on the farm mentioned in [my last post](/posts/set-up-my-studio), I released music under the name Sacra Monet (which eventually morphed into sacramo thanks to [CCRMA](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/)’s username length restrictions). I recently migrated those releases from streaming services to Bandcamp and have decided to revive the moniker for instrumental releases such as this. 

I promised to keep it brief, and I intend to deliver on that promise, so give a listen to *Music for Transparency* if you’d like and maybe an old track or two while you’re there. I’ll be right back here for number /#11.
