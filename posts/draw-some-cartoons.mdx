I told you I'd be back soon, didn't I? No rest for the weary while there are still items to be crossed out on the list, and there are a *lot* of items to be crossed out on the list. Catch up from the beginning of the Endeavor with [my first post](/posts/document-everything).

One approach that has proven fruitful both in addressing open questions around specific tasks and making the overall Endeavor feel more manageable is finding entries that can compound on each other. So instead of 29 distinct tasks to complete, I have some lesser number of total projects, and the individual list items capture the project at distinct phases or evolutions. It's still a large number, mind you, but every bit helps when you're on a time table. 

That's the long and short of how #7 “Draw some cartoons” has taken its final shape. I've been doing my fair share of childhood wish fulfillment thus far along the journey, and it is true that I drew a handful of cartoons as a kid who dearly loved *Captain Underpants* and wanted to emulate George and Harold (though ~~mother~~ editor was unable to locate the evidence). But all of that came back to me *after* the task made its way onto the list, which happened in a rather more circuitous manner.

Like every task, there's what I *intended* to do and what I actually end up doing. That's not a value judgment; typically, the place I wind up is more interesting than the place I started. For this task, I thought I might take a day and make some silly drawings and throw them up in a new tab on [Supchuck](/supchuck) since it's already got that hand drawn aesthetic and seemed the best home for an extra dash of nose-thumbing. What wound up happening is that once I had cartoons on the brain, they just kind of materialized by accident. 

Every good blog post needs an image, and many times, the right image is obvious: a photo of a thing you made, a promotional image of Jason Lee. Self-evident, really. But sometimes you want to make a joke and there's no ready-made visual aid, like heaven's [“No Red Canoe”](/posts/build-an-audience) policy or why [Morehead-Cain Scholars like medical school](/posts/apply-to-spc) (it makes sense in context, I promise). Who knew cartoons were such a useful tool?

So that's it, that's the task. I'm going to keep drawing silly cartoons when a post warrants. It makes the drawings more meaningful and the posts more humorous, so win-win. If that feels anti-climactic, well, here's a cartoon:

## Life, Comics, and Everything

If the entire premise of this series did not serve to make my age readily apparent, it's funny to think that something as simple as having had the experience of reading the Sunday comics in an actual, honest-to-goodness, print-circulated newspaper sets a lower bound on it. If you know, you know, but it was always far more satisfying to copy a full-color strip onto Silly Putty than the black-and-white dailies. <span className="text-[#77FFE4] text-sm">[Though I never really knew what to do with it after the fact. Save it to read in the mirror on Monday?]</span>

My favorite comic as a kid was one that I never actually saw in print because its entire fifteen-year run concluded more than two years before I was born. I speak, of course, of [*The Far Side*](https://webassets.thefarside.com/). Lacking other means, my main exposure to Gary Larson's brand of humor was the 10th anniversary collection, [The Prehistory of The Far Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prehistory_of_The_Far_Side), which I still enjoy revisiting to this day. The volume is captivating in equal measure for the insights it provides into his process as well as the gags contained within. 

Despite my talk of comics in print, I am nothing if not a child of the internet, so fittingly, many of my favorite strips are webcomics. While the art form is still very much alive (particularly on Reddit), there was an undeniable golden age from the mid-2000s to the mid-2010s. Foremost amongst its peers was (and is) [Saturday Morning Breakfast Cereal](https://www.smbc-comics.com/), but also [Hyperbole and a Half](https://hyperboleandahalf.blogspot.com/), [The Oatmeal](https://theoatmeal.com/) and [Cyanide & Happiness](https://explosm.net/) (in my edgier teenage years). Not to mention that every computer scientist has read their fair share of [XKCD](https://xkcd.com/). And while I discovered [The Perry Bible Fellowship](https://pbfcomics.com/) more recently, I'd be remiss not to include it, too. Collectively, they are probably the single greatest influence on my own sense of humor. 

I alluded to the fact that despite all this apparent meaning around cartoons, the task made its way onto the list in roundabout fashion, courtesy of another cartoonist who, like Larson, called it quits in 1995 (albeit December 31st rather than January 1): Bill Watterson. I had friends who were big fans of *Calvin & Hobbes* growing up, but it never made its way into my personal childhood canon. Over the years, I picked up various tidbits about its author, Watterson, given his internet-hero status, like his steadfast refusal to compromise his artistic vision for the mountains of cash that he could have made by licensing the characters for merchandise, but it was a recent discovery that brought all this to the fore: a [speech he gave](https://web.mit.edu/jmorzins/www/C-H-speech.html) at Kenyon College for Commencement in 1990. It's well worth the time it takes to give it a read.

There's a fair bit of meaning-construction underpinning this Endeavor (if that was not already obvious), as are your twenties as a whole, and I've spent the better part of a decade now figuring out how to live life on my own terms. To read that speech and see so many of my own thoughts reflected back at me on the page is a particular kind of heartening.

> To invent your own life's meaning is not easy, but it's still allowed, and I think you'll be happier for the trouble.

From there, it was only a short jump back to all those other cartoons that I've loved through the years. When you realize that so many of your idols make silly little drawings, it's hard to resist the urge to draw some of your own, quality be damned. You'll just have to accept my apologies if stick figures are not to your taste.
