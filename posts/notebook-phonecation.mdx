## Fill a Notebook

I made mention of my proclivities for assembling collections in my [photo post](/posts/shoot-a-roll-of-film), but the most inexplicable (and inadvertent) of them has always been the large volume of empty notebooks that I have accumulated through the years. I’m a sucker for quality stationary and never short on fantasies of all the things I could write in a new notebook (and why all the ones I already have don’t suit that purpose near as well). But when it comes time to actually *use* a notebook? Well, I couldn’t sully it like that, now could I? I think I’ve been scared off by all the times I started using one for a particular project that never made it past the first page, as though I’d never been introduced to the concept of repurposing a notebook. Instead, I was much more likely to make use of the Notes app or write on a tablet where, for better or worse, my thoughts felt more ephemeral.

Like it did for so many of my activities the past several months, the Endeavor provided ample encouragement to finally face my fear of the blank page. If ever I was to have a project worth dedicating an entire notebook to, it was this one, being both expansive enough to warrant the page count and significant enough to properly archive. I divided my notebook into three main sections to give myself a structure to work from: one with a page per task for planning, one with a page per day for reflection and intention-setting, and one for everything else.

I learned several things in the process of working my way cover-to-cover. First and foremost was that it was a mistake to pick such a large-format notebook to begin with. Sure, it has gravitas, but that gravitas pales in comparisons to the flashbacks of AP FRQs that I’d get every time my hand cramped up. Second, I’m not sure if I’m much of a journal person, despite representing the largest section of the notebook by far. I did find it easier to habituate a daily practice like journaling than to develop new practices around ideation, though (hello [incrementalism](/posts/embrace-the-incremental), my old friend). I might feel differently if I didn’t have these posts as an aid in solidifying my thoughts, which conveniently brings me to my last point: I still found it quite difficult to write down anything that didn’t feel final. The few times I did utilize scratch pages to organize thought-in-progress were definitely helpful because the act of defining the problem explicitly was often enough to let my brain do the rest, so I’d be well-served in getting over that hurdle.

## Take a Phone-cation

> Show me a person who doesn’t use their phone too much, and I’ll show you a liar - Plato

For starters, I don’t use my phone too much, but it certainly wouldn’t hurt to make a concerted effort at reducing my screen time in favor of activities that are more *productive*. In my ~~scramble to figure out tasks I could finish on time~~ careful consideration of tasks to include on the list, I deemed a phone-lite week a worthwhile experiment that stood to benefit all the rest and set out to eliminate all non-essential uses like social media and games. 

I don’t have anything ground breaking to report back, and for a while, I struggled to find something to say about using my phone less that felt worth saying. It’s not like there’s some grand debate around the merit of smartphones. We’re all pretty well aware the toll of the attention economy by this point. Yes, I’m happy having not used my phone to waste time I already knew I was wasting. Did I necessarily always fill that time productively? No, but I am confident I made better use of it overall. And when combined with all my [comfort zone departing](/posts/leave-my-comfort-zone) last week, it was quite the powerful force for breaking routine.

When I told my wife that I was going to turn these two entries into a double post, she said that it made sense because they both are about becoming comfortable with empty space, and I’d just like to apologize for depriving you of the truly insightful one in this relationship in all my posting. A lot of the Endeavor has been concerned with the journey from zero to one, but it’s important to learn to accept the zero as the starting and even the occasional necessity of subtracting something to get back to it.
