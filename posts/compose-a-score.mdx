## Transparent Tuning

It's no secret that I like a quality walk. For one, you don't move to Brooklyn if you're averse to the notion, and further, you certainly wouldn't engineer a [walking brainstorm system](/posts/engineer-an-ai-system) unless you're an active practitioner. There are any number of walks that you become well-versed in when living in the city, like the “hurry-up-there's-a-train”, the “slow-down-there-will-be-another”, or the “oh-my-god-it's-finally-not-\{freezing, roasting\}-and-I-want-to-be-outside.” What's true of most walks (of the solo variety) is that they're often improved by music. 

Music preference is a matter of taste, of course, but some walks lend themselves better to certain types of music than others. For example, I've always tended towards instrumental music when brainstorming because lyrics tend to draw too much of my focus. And then, of course, there's the matter of listening arrangement. One AirPod or two? How about noise-cancellation? Personally, I like to experience the city around me (as opposed to blocking it out), so I usually opt for one in, one out, but it's hard to argue that you're not losing something when listening in mono. 

It was on a walk to the [Brooklyn Botanical Garden](https://www.bbg.org/) that I came up with a unique solution, but one that's only suitable for specific types of music. I found that by listening to the music at a low volume with transparency turned on, the music and background blend would inevitably merge into a single amalgam of sound. My initial experiment was with Sigur Rós, which is still a favorite for this purpose. The key feature of any potential candidates is ample sonic space for the outside world to fill.

Instead of repurposing existing music for this purpose, I thought it would make for an interesting prompt to attempt to compose a piece with a transparent listening environment in mind. It's also no coincidence that the notion pairs particularly well with my [MindWalk](https://github.com/sslowe/mindwalk) tool. So that's the impetus behind the descriptively titled [*Music for Transparency*](https://sacramonet.bandcamp.com/track/music-for-transparency). It's site-unspecified music that becomes [site-specific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site-specific_art) with every listen.

It's been a while since I tried my hand at a score, the last attempt being a collaboration with my Router cofounder, Scott, for the third installation of his [Resurrection Machine](https://informationarcade.com/resurrection/) series. It's a closely-related but distinct skillset to songwriting that uses alternate approaches for similar ends, albeit on different time scales. For this work, I found the operative term was **restraint**. I spent far more time carving away bits of sounds than I ever did adding them, and the initial versions *still* managed to drown out any environmental sound.

Back during the time I spent on the farm (also mentioned in [my studio post](/posts/set-up-my-studio)), I released music under the name Sacra Monet (which eventually morphed into sacramo thanks to [CCRMA](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/)'s username length restrictions). I recently migrated that EP from streaming services to [Bandcamp](https://sacramonet.bandcamp.com/album/slowe-byrne) and have decided to revive the moniker for instrumental releases such as this. 

I promised to keep it brief, and I intend to deliver on that promise, so give a listen to [*Music for Transparency*](https://sacramonet.bandcamp.com/track/music-for-transparency) if you'd like and maybe an old track or two while you're there. I'll be right back here for number /#11.
