Another day, another task coming off the list. Even better, we’re finally knocking on the door of #20, so the end of the Endeavor is coming into view. If you missed out on any of the story so far, feel free to jump back to [my introductory post](/posts/document-everything).

Though by now I'm well into the back half of the series, this task was the very first I added to the list way back when. One of my motivating principles was getting around to long overdue goals, so naturally the first place my mind went was to something I’ve intended to do for twenty-some years now.

## Cue *Ancient Voices*

I was introduced to *Survivor* by my aunt, and I can recall watching the show as early as season 12, *Survivor: Panama* (though it’s likely I was watching even earlier because I own a buff for the prior season, *Guatemala*). In those pre-DVR days, I would race to the TV at promptly 8’o’clock and proceed to call Aunt Nan each and every commercial break to discuss whatever we had just seen.

The heroes and villains of those early seasons were larger-than-life figures to my younger self. Along with most *Survivor* fans, I cheered on the likes of Ozzy and J.T., but true to my childhood tendencies writ large, I **loved** a villain. *Survivor* seasons are very much relics of the time they were produced, and it’s hard to ignore the misogynistic streak of the early years with the benefit of hindsight, which I say as a necessary disclaimer before averring that my favorite player at the time was none other than Russell Hantz. <span className="text-[#77FFE4] text-sm">[Despite his boorish behavior, he had a nose for idols like a bloodhound.]</span>

From the beginning, I made clear my aim to make it on the show one day. I wanted nothing more as a child than to be an explorer, in no small part due to the heady combination of *The Road to El Dorado*, *National Treasure*, and *Sahara*. I was, of course, ignorant to the fact that most of that work had already been taken care of (not to mention the imperialistic undertones because, well, I was eight), but the desire for adventure remained. And boy, did *Survivor* stoke that flame.

Fortunately for me, I had an aunt who was ready and willing to indulge those fantasies, and in 2006, I had the chance to star in my very own production of the show. On a trip to the Outer Banks with my other aunt, Gaynell (of [photo post lore](/posts/shoot-a-roll-of-film)), a storm rolled in that threatened to make a wash of the whole weekend. Aunt Nan came speeding in from Durham with a plan (and dog) in tow, and *Survivor: Hatteras* was born. From the comfort of our hotel room, we competed in challenges, hunted for idols, and survived on exile island (the bath tub). If early *Survivor* seasons have the occasional cringe-inducing line, try contending with your nine year-old self on screen. What I can say is I sure knew how to play the heel.

My love of *Survivor* has continued unabated to the present day, despite missing a few seasons when I got a high school girlfriend and went off to college. In fact, my dreams of making it on *Survivor* were my main coping strategies over the course of my Outward Bound experience (as mandated by the Morehead-Cain), the stories of which certainly warrant a post all their own. Marissa and I did eventually get around to a hearty rewatch in 2020 and have been keeping up ever since, all the while scheming on my eventual application video. <span className="text-[#77FFE4] text-sm">[And trying to keep track of the *Survivor: Hatteras* footage, which I always knew would be my ace in the hole.]</span>

With *Survivor* firmly on the brain since the outset of the Endeavor, things came to a head around my birthday, when I threw a *Survivor*-themed party filled with challenges and idols of my very own. Since I was still planning my video at the time, I made sure to capture ample footage of the proceedings in case they turned out to be helpful in the edit bay. It was ultimately unnecessary, but I’d be remiss if this photo never saw the light of day:

As I debated when in the course of the Endeavor to work on my audition tape, I anticipated a drawn-out process of storyboarding and editing. Imagine my surprise, then, when I found that it came together relatively quickly (especially when most tasks end up taking *longer* than initially expected). I started digging into the audition tips provided by Jeff and casting, and the number one tip was not to read from a script since it can hide your personality, which is the main thing they’re looking for. So I jotted down a few notes on what I wanted to say and just started practicing. Seventeen takes later, and I finally managed to convey my message clearly and succinctly. My one pitfall was not accounting for the exact clip I wanted to pull from *Survivor: Hatteras*, which meant that I had to chop my final “Thanks!” awkwardly, but it was a clean take otherwise. 

I warned you in my last post that I wasn’t sure if I could bring myself to make my audition video public, and turns out that trepidation was warranted because I’m keeping it unlisted for now, though I will admit I’d be less embarrassed to share it than I would *Survivor: Hatteras* in its entirety (and not just because I mooned the camera in the final challenge). I’ll make a deal, though: if I get on the show, I’ll make the video public for all to enjoy. The audition tape, that is. Seems like a fair trade.
